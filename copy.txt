Title: The interesting part starts when the learner gets stuck.

I started my career in digital education designing self-paced courses, interactive lessons and video-based learning. As my role grew into content strategy and Product Ownership, I became responsible not only for what we taught, but for how learners encountered it, progressed through it and responded to it.

Most of the audience I have designed for has been in the US. I use learner interviews, surveys, usability testing and behavioral data alongside learning outcomes to understand both where people struggle and why.

One of my favorite projects came from questioning the structure of a core learning experience rather than simply adding more content. A more progressive format more than doubled completion and was later adopted for content production at scale.

AI has also become a very practical part of how I work. I use it to prototype interactive experiences, animations, short educational video formats, landing pages and internal tools. What interests me is being able to take a learning idea much further on my own, test it quickly, and only then decide where significant engineering or production time is really needed.

03 · SELECTED WORK
=====================================

Title: A few learning problems I've worked on.

--- 01 Redesigning the learning format ---
Subtitle: From 10% completion to 21% by changing how the skill was taught.
Metric: 2.1× completion
Tags: Learning Experience Design · Instructional Design · Learning Analytics · Experimentation
Scope: Product Owner · Learning experience & content
The problem: A core learning experience was losing too many learners before completion. The obvious response could have been to add more material. Instead, I questioned the structure itself.
What I did: I helped validate a more progressive format that broke a complex skill into smaller guided steps and gave learners more support as they moved toward the full task. We treated it as a learning-design hypothesis and looked at completion, satisfaction and early retention rather than relying on whether the new format simply felt better.
What happened: Completion increased from 10% to 21%. Top satisfaction ratings increased by 14 percentage points. Day 1 retention rose from 55% to 67%. The format was then commissioned for content production at scale.

--- 02 Finding the learning that happens outside the product ---
Subtitle: Sometimes the workaround tells you more than the funnel does.
Metric: 1/3 of learners used external video
Tags: Learner Research · Behavioral Analytics · Discovery · Learning Strategy
Scope: Product Owner · Learner research & engagement roadmap
The problem: Behavioral data could show us where learners disengaged, but it could not always tell us what they felt was missing.
What I did: I combined learner interviews, surveys, usability testing and behavioral analysis to understand how people were actually learning, including what they did once they left the product.
What we found: Roughly one-third of learners were supplementing the product with external video content. That changed the question. Instead of only asking how we could keep them inside the product, we could ask what they were leaving to find.
What happened: The research fed directly into the engagement roadmap and how we thought about future learning formats.

--- 03 Personalizing the first learning experience ---
Subtitle: Different goals should not always lead to the same first lesson.
Metric: 3 signals shape the first lesson
Tags: Personalization · Onboarding · Learner Goals · Experimentation
Scope: Education Content Manager · Onboarding & personalization
The problem: Learners arrived with different goals, preferences and contexts, while the early experience treated too many of them the same way.
What I did: I designed and tested onboarding approaches using learning goals, content preferences and location to adapt the experience. Success measures were defined before launch so each iteration could be evaluated rather than judged by intuition.
What I learned: Personalization works best when every question earns its place. If an answer does not meaningfully change what the learner sees or does next, it is worth asking why we need the question at all.

--- 04 Scaling learning content without scaling chaos ---
Subtitle: 260+ learning modules in one release, with the production system evolving around them.
Metric: 260+ modules in one release
Tags: Content Operations · Localization · Learning Systems · Scale
Scope: Content Manager → Product Owner · Content, localization & engineering
The problem: Large multilingual learning releases involve much more than creating the lesson itself. Production, localization, quality assurance, contributors and release readiness all have to move together.
What I did: I owned releases end to end, coordinated international content and localization, and later worked with content and engineering to rebuild the production tooling behind them.
What happened: One quarterly release delivered 260+ learning modules. The learning-content library grew by 160%. A separate 61-item Japan and Korea release was built around locally validated learner needs. The redesigned production workflow later reduced end-to-end content delivery time by 70%.

04 · AI IN LEARNING
=====================================

Title: I use AI to get learning ideas into people's hands faster.

Intro: AI is part of my working process, but I am less interested in generating content for the sake of generating content. I use it to move from an idea to something I can see, click, test or produce, often before asking engineering or design for significant time. I am especially interested in learning formats that previously would have been too slow or expensive to create at scale.

--- 01 Interactive chord builder (Interactive learning · Live) ---
An example of the kind of learning format I find exciting: turning a concept-heavy topic into something learners can explore directly instead of only reading an explanation.
Interactive tools give learners immediate ways to inspect relationships, test ideas and move between explanation and practice.
Link: View live tool → https://www.skoove.com/en/tools/piano-chords

--- 02 Short educational video at scale (Content production · Experimentation) ---
I am exploring repeatable AI-assisted workflows for short, focused educational videos that can be produced in much larger volumes without becoming generic or disposable.
The interesting challenge is not generating more video. It is preserving the explanation, pacing and usefulness that make a short learning asset worth watching.

--- 03 AI dubbing for learning localization (Localization · In production) ---
Evaluating AI dubbing for localized learning video, including quality, workflow and the human review required before publication.
Expanded: Video lessons are filmed once, in English. AI dubbing lets the same lessons speak a new language in days rather than through a full studio cycle. For me this is a good example of where AI can remove production constraints while human judgment stays important for accuracy and learning quality.
What I took away:
- The last 20% is still human. AI handles the bulk of the voiceover, but subject terms, names and timing against what is happening on screen need an editing pass before anything ships.
- Localization is a learning decision, not a translation task. Markets are chosen from learner data first, then dubbed, not the other way round.
- Cheap enough to test. Cost per localized minute drops far enough to validate demand in a market before committing to a whole course.
