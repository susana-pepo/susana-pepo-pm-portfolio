01 · About

🌱 I build for the person who almost left.

Most products lose people quietly. Someone signs up, hits a moment that does not fit them, and never comes back. I spend my time finding those moments and removing them, through discovery, experiments, and a roadmap that is honest about what actually moves activation, conversion, and retention.

At Learnfield I am Product Owner across two squads: the Trala violin app and the Skoove piano content team. The role sits where product, data, and commercial meet. In one week I might refine epics with engineering, read out a paywall A/B test in Mixpanel, and negotiate licensing terms with a music publisher. I was trained in negotiation directly by our CEO, and I am the direct interlocutor with publishers and rights organizations on the terms that decide what the product can offer.

What I like most is the range. I translate ambiguous commercial requirements into something a team can build, then I follow it all the way through to whether it worked.

03 · Selected Work

💡 Four projects that shaped how I work.

01

Song Discovery

From a buried catalogue to the number one way users find songs.

Product Discovery A/B Testing User Research Funnel Optimization

Problem

Skoove had a large song catalogue, but discovery ran entirely through curated collections. That worked for new users and frustrated returning learners who knew exactly what they wanted to play.

What I did

Contributed across discovery, scoping, and post-launch analysis for both the Song Search MVP and the Favorites feature. Coordinated QA across platforms and tracked results in Mixpanel. For Favorites I designed unmoderated usability research in Maze, led KPI tracking, and wrote the post-experiment report.

Outcome

Search became the number one discovery method in the Songs tab, ahead of suggested songs. Favorites hit its primary KPI: 30% of premium users who visited Songs saved at least one. A search-paywall A/B test fed a strategic decision on free-trial access.

02

Paywall & Onboarding Experiments

Turning first impressions into paying users.

Subscription A/B Testing Onboarding Conversion Experiment Design

Problem

The paywall and the first-run experience were doing less than they could. New users arrived with very different goals, and everyone saw the same thing.

What I did

Ran subscription A/B tests on RevenueCat and Mixpanel, owning each from hypothesis and metric design through to read-out and follow-up. Personalized onboarding with location-based content and questionnaire variants on genre and learning goal, so the product adapted to why each person came.

Outcome

Projected revenue per paywall visitor rose 28.7%. The onboarding changes contributed to 14% year-on-year revenue growth.

03

The Commercial Behind the Catalogue

The licensing and negotiation work that lets people learn the songs they actually listen to.

Commercial Negotiation Licensing Rights Strategy Stakeholder Management

Problem

Every popular song is wrapped in a web of publishers, performance rights organizations, and territory rules. Putting that music inside a learning app, at a cost that works, is a negotiation before it is a feature.

What I did

Led the negotiation that unlocked the Disney Music Group catalogue, our largest Disney piano collection against the competition. Worked publisher by publisher on multi-year terms, royalty mechanics, rights clearance, and dispute resolution, as the direct interlocutor for the company. Trained in negotiation by the CEO and CPO.

Outcome

A contemporary, high-demand catalogue users want, with the licensing cost ratio improved from 18% to 14.7% of revenue.

04

Content Operations at Scale

Growing the catalogue 160% while keeping quality and licensing coverage intact.

Content Ops Process Design Localization Cross-functional Delivery

Problem

Running content for an app at this size means shipping constantly, new songs, seasonal collections, and localized versions, without letting quality slip. The workflows were manual and fragmented.

What I did

Worked on three levels at once. Rebuilt the internal content tooling to reduce engineer dependency. Ran bulk localization across 7+ languages with coordinated QA, including market pushes into Japan and Korea. Built the licensing foundation, partner pages, a content-tactics matrix, and rights-ownership workflows, now used across Engineering, Content, and Marketing.

Outcome

The catalogue grew 160% with licensing coverage and quality held intact. End-to-end process time down 70%. Quarterly releases shipping up to 260+ songs at a time, on schedule, with content decisions tied to real usage data.
